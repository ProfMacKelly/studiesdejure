# Copy of enthymemes

Also, many argument-based statements contain implicit or implied premises (assertions presumed to be true by the arguer), which are called **enthymemes**.\[\*][^1]&#x20;

**Enthymemes** are also common in daily conversation and in judicial opinions. Here are a couple of examples:\[\*][^2]

> * Drunk driving hurts innocent people.
> * Therefore, drunk driving is wrong.



There is a missing link in the chain of reasoning: Hurting innocent people is wrong_._ The position that hurting innocent people is wrong is implied; its truth is simply presumed. Thinkers need to be on the lookout for enthymemes because sometimes, the presumed truth is not actually true. The presumed truths can lead to a fundamentally flawed conclusion.

Here is another example of an enthymeme\[\*][^3]:

> * Governor Johnson wants to reduce government regulation and oversight
> * Therefore, Governor Johnson is an anarchist

Here, the implicit presumption is that a person who wants to reduce government regulation and oversight is an anarchist. Certainly, this presumption cannot be universally true. Thus, the result—Governor Johnson is an anarchist _because_ he wants to reduce regulation and oversight—is fundamentally flawed.
