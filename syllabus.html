Concordia University Mission

Concordia University is a Lutheran higher education community committed to helping students develop in mind, body, and spirit for service to Christ in the Church and in the world.

Program/Department Mission or Vision

The Batterman School of Business emboldens a global network of transformational and collaborative Christian leaders, dedicated faculty, entrepreneurial students, and active alumni who come together to create maximum social impact through ethical practice, innovation, and service.

Led by our Christ-centered values, we believe that the best business leaders demonstrate both uncompromising compassion and unmatched competitive skills. Because of this, our education develops students to master the “triple bottom line:” people, planet, profit. As a Concordia business graduate, you can expect to stand out as you develop a genuine, ethical mindset alongside a strength of business acumen that is unmatched in the market.

This course offers an introduction to the American Legal System and several specific legal topics including, but not limited to, criminal law, torts, employment law and discrimination, agency, contracts and sales.

► Generative AI & Plagiarism

When generative AI

 is used to represent one’s own work, it is considered plagiarism. AI tools do not “author” their generated output, and the output does not contain original work or ideas. To explain further, the following discussion is a selection from the CUWAA’s AI Task Force’s Report on Generative AI in Higher Ed (2023) by Sam Speckhard (Taskforce Chair), Jonathan Balsman, Robert Bruss, Chris Cunningham, Kathleen Kannass, Matthew Lee, Joshua Locklair, and Matthew Mac Kelly:

[Generative AI tool] designers collect, or “scrape,” large quantities of data from the internet. This data is usually in the form of written text, and it can come from public or government websites (e.g., Wikipedia, cdc.gov), social media (Twitter posts, Reddit, Facebook, etc.), personal blogs, news sites, or really anywhere that a person could visit on the web. These pieces of data are organized into grouped units known as “tokens.”  The designers curate some data but otherwise integrate the data into a learning model. The model examines this incomprehensibly massive amount of written text and starts to find patterns in word sequencing. Some “tokens” of data are prioritized in specific context settings to help the AI determine likelihoods of optimal outcomes when given context through a prompt. The designers code it to recognize and answer prompts based on those patterns. This is typically done through responding to information with new text. The AI [makes inferences from these inputs] and tries to respond mimicking the patterns it has picked up along the way. * * * Ultimately, the model is a word prediction system. Entering a prompt generates a response that largely infers what the user intends, predicting the most likely word to follow each word it sends out to you (based on all of the data it has accumulated). Principally, this is like what people experience with predictive text when drafting web search engine queries, text messages, and emails, but on an infinitesimally smaller scale.  However, despite the technological sophistication, these applications still include less likely options within the algorithm to simulate creativity. No matter what inferences the application makes, it generates responses in ways that tend to convey a high degree of confidence and accuracy, leading unassuming readers to believe the AI tool actually “knows” [or understands] what it is talking about.

Because AI tools do not have the capacity to “know” or understand/comprehend, even if they cite purported sources, the “source” content does not always reflect what the AI tools claimed. Moreover, this lack of understanding prevents tools cannot discern false information from accurate information.

Academic Integrity Policy

Concordia University expects all students to display honest, ethical behavior at all times and under all circumstances. Academic dishonesty is defined as follows:

Cheating

[

I]ncludes, but is not limited to: a) the use of unauthorized assistance in taking any type of test or completing any type of classroom assignment; b) assisting another student in cheating on a test or class assignment, including impersonation of another student.

Plagiarism

[

I]ncludes, but is not limited to: a) failure to give full and clear acknowledgement of the source of any idea that is not your own; b) handing in the same assignment for two different courses without the consent of the instructors.

 

Fabrication

The forgery, alteration, or misuse of any University academic document, record, or instrument of identification.

Academic Misconduct

Intentionally or recklessly interfering with teaching, research, and/or other academic functions.

Sanctions

Faculty members who find evidence of academic dishonesty have sole discretion to determine the penalty, using their professional judgment. This can include a failing grade in the course, or removal of the student from the course. Additional sanctions will be imposed when a student is found to have violated the academic integrity policy more than once; these sanctions may include suspension or expulsion from the university.

Course Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

        1. Exhibit a working knowledge of the American legal system and how it impacts business, as well as developing a fundamental understanding of the legal doctrines and theories of liability studied;
        2. Identify the legal dispute resolution alternatives to litigation and explain the role that Christian peacemaking principles play in dispute resolution;
        3. Demonstrate analytical and critical thinking skills by identifying and evaluating legal issues and concepts that arise in the context of business decision-making;
        4. Apply substantive legal theory and ethical decision-making considerations that enhance a manager’s practical ability to recognize and respond to legal challenges in a business context; 
        5. Articulate ways in which legal acumen adds value to a business through risk management, business planning, and problem-solving.
